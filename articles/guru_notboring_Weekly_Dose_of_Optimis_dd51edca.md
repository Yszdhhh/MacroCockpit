#### [(1) SpaceX’s Mechazilla Catches an Orbital Starship](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/2121715216597827756)

Humanity is one giant leap closer to being multiplanetary.

Over the weekend on Starship Flight 15, Ship 42 launched from Starbase, Texas, reached orbit, deployed a batch of Starlink satellites, spent more than 22 hours in space, survived fiery hypersonic re-entry, flipped upright, and dropped cleanly into Mechazilla’s chopsticks. The SpaceX team made one of the greatest engineering feats in human history look easy.

SpaceX has already caught Super Heavy boosters before, which was already absurd. Catching the orbital upper stage , the part that goes into orbit carrying the payload to complete a mission, was the final boss of rapid, full reusability. The final boss is dead.

While the booster is more expensive at around 70% of Starship’s cost, it is somewhat easier to reuse given that it carries the upper stage up and comes right back. Having proven that the booster is reusable, the upper stage was like 90% of the marginal cost per flight. Having to make a new one each time would leave Starship with a hard floor around $30-40M per launch, but making it reusable means that the marginal cost of launch drops to something like $2-10M per launch, or $20-100/kg versus Falcon 9’s $1,000/kg internal cost.

SpaceX still has plenty of work ahead on heat-shield durability and turnaround time before Starships are flying as repeatably as planes, of course, but the goal is to send up about two a week by the end of 2027, hundreds per year by 2029 ,and multiple per day beyond that. They’ll carry satellites and data centers and drug factories and eventually all sorts of things we’ll need to thrive in space and on other worlds.

For now, just watch the slow-motion footage above and marvel. Humans rock.

#### (2) [Leukemia Vaccine Cuts Risk of Death by 41% in Phase 3 Trial](https://www.washingtonpost.com/health/2026/11/17/leukemia-vaccine-passes-key-trial-adding-breakthrough-year-cancer-drugs/)

Video created with [Runway](https://runway.com/?utm_source=notboring&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=notboring-2026-11-20)

At this point, you know how we feel about cancer here at the Dose. Hate it.

It’s been a big year for us cancer haters, and the streak continues.

On Tuesday, [SELLAS Life Sciences](https://ir.sellaslifesciences.com/news/News-Details/2026/SELLAS-Life-Sciences-Announces-Positive-Pivotal-Phase-3-REGAL-Trial-Data-Showing-Immunotherapeutic-Cancer-Vaccine-Galinpepimut-S-GPS-Demonstrated-a-Statistically-Significant-and-Clinically-Meaningful-Improvement-in-Overall-Survival-in-Patients-with-A/default.aspx) and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center announced positive topline results from the pivotal Phase 3 REGAL trial of galinpepimut-S (GPS), an immunotherapeutic vaccine for acute myeloid leukemia (AML). AML is a brutal blood cancer with roughly a 33% five-year survival rate that kills around 11,500 Americans every year.

The trial followed 126 patients whose AML had relapsed, who had fought their way back into a second (or later) remission, and who weren't eligible for a stem-cell transplant. The patients were a high-risk group with very few options left.

And GPS extended their lives. Patients who received GPS alone (with no chemo alongside it) had a median overall survival of 24.0 months, compared to 14.7 months for patients on investigator's choice of best available treatment. That's more than **nine extra months of life** and a **41% reduction in the risk of death**.

Huge. While it’s not a total cure, we seem to be entering a period in which a number of therapies are able to extend patients’ lives while researchers around the world work to find more permanent cures. Meaning, an extra nine months both nine months and a call option on much longer.

This isn’t the first promising vaccine this year. Back in [Dose #207](https://www.notboring.co/), we covered Merck and Moderna’s personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine that cut recurrence or death by 42% in Phase 3. The exciting thing about GPS isn’t custom-built from an individual tumor like that one, but an off-the-shelf peptide vaccine, which should make it faster, cheaper, and more widely available if approved. Even better, the WT1 protein shows up in a number of blood cancers and solid tumors, so GPS may be effective against more than just AML.

#### (3) [Palmer Luckey Reveals SLAB, a 1,000-Year Floating Seastead](https://x.com/AtomsNotBits/status/2122864700866351508)

Well, here’s a happy challenge we might have to deal with. If people live a lot longer, where are we going to but them all?

Bah gaw that’s Palmer Luckey’s music!

Fresh off announcing Warhound and a drumbeat of positive Anduril announcements, Luckey announced on X (to preempt a journalist working on the story) that he’s “developing a seastead called SLAB with a 1,000 year design life.”

To get ahead of people thinking he might just be another guy doing an ocean data center thing (this is “way cooler” than that, Palmer said that he’s actually been privately funding and building SLAB since selling Oculus to Facebook for $2 billion in 2014. He and some friends capitalized it at a $1 billion valuation, and put more money in recently.

The idea is that SLAB will be a massive off-shore floating industrial platform engineered to last a thousand years that’s meant to be cheaper than land while operating outside the regulatory morass that chokes American heavy industry. On SLAB, future Palmers may build factories for metal processing, desalination, oil refining, chemical engineering, and frontier medical research and treatment.

Thousand Year Luckey Empire.

If those two parallel electric universes do even one-tenth of what they’re planning to do, we’re going to need a lot of metal. Challenge is: the world spends something like $14 billion searching for new mineral deposits, but major discoveries have dropped ~70% over the past decade. We’ve already pulled out the easy stuff, and it’s going to take new technology to get at the harder stuff economically.

Luckily, as you may know, we are in a golden age of technology, and our friends and not boring capital portfolio company Earth AI are using it to find metals.. This week, the company announced that its Alpheus AI model found a continuous copper, gold, and platinum-group metals system stretching more than 3 kilometers at the Fontenoy/Meridian project in New South Wales, Australia. There’s some Tungsten in there too. Roman showed me one of the rocks - the Tungsten shows up as black spots and is easy to mistake for nothing.

Explorers actually drilled the site back in 1969 and again in 1997, but they thought it was just some scattered pockets that wouldn’t have been economical to mind. Back in January, Earth AI turned its models on the old data, and the models recognized that they weren’t looking at discontinuous “black smoker” pockets; they predicted that it was actually a massive magma-filled fault line.

Five weeks later, Earth AI started drilling (it owns its own rigs and labs, so it didn’t have to wait for slow and expensive contractors; [vertical integration](https://www.notboring.co/p/vertical-integrators) ftw). It’s drilled 35 holes and traced mineralization from 30m to 478m deep across the 3km seam, with hints that it might actually be 8km long.

As we covered in our [Deep Dive on Earth AI](https://www.notboring.co/p/earth-ai), discovery is just the first step in a long process towards getting metal out of the ground, but with its rigs, it’s moving towards feasibility more quickly than other explorers traditionally could.

I think the company has the potential to be one of the world’s most beautiful businesses as these discoveries prove out, sell off, and turn into cash which buys more rigs and allows Earth AI to prove out the other 4,500 targets on its list. This week’s news is a golden step in the right direction.
