#### (1) [Alpöge & Claude Prove 167-year-old Riemann Hypothesis](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/riemann-hypothesis-anthropic-math-levent-alpoge-75351bcb)

Ben Cohen and Joshua Robinson for WSJ; video created with [Runway](https://runway.com/?utm_source=notboring&utm_medium=newsletter&utm_campaign=notboring-2026-11-27)

Last Friday may have been the most important day in mathematics in the past two centuries, although I’m sure there will be many more important ones to come.

Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge dropped a 17-million-line formal proof in Lean of not just the Riemann Hypothesis, posed by Bernhard Riemann in 1859 and the crown jewel of the seven $1 million Millennium Prize Problems, but the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions.

Alpöge developed his proof over six weeks alongside an unreleased frontier version of Claude. Within 24 hours, on Saturday afternoon, the [Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative jointly certified](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) that both the theorem statement and the proof are valid after dozens of volunteers compiled the codebase and ran it through multiple independent, formally verified proof checkers: “We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct.”

There was a bit of controversy after [Alpöge joked](https://x.com/__alpoge__/status/2124987173439209561?a=DzfkA-7je2BmPgSRAbE&s=20) that he had a 250,000 page version of it or something (I don’t get the joke) but what’s true is that no human can read the proof yet. here is no natural-language paper, only machine-verified code that Terence Tao noted appears to draw on “the full power of modern developments on the Langlands program and arithmetic geometry” (and as one number theorist put it, “basically contains all number theory produced in the last 50 years”). Whatever, he cracked it. Or they cracked it. Steven Strogatz called the duo “a very skilled AI jockey riding a very fast AI horse.” Welcome to Centaur Math. Lean has checked that the proof is mathematically airtight before humans can understand it.

Oh, and both Anthropic and OpenAI also posted AI-generated proofs of VP ≠ VNP, which is the algebraic cousin of the longstanding P vs. NP problem, which has been open since 1979, may invalidate a lot of cryptography if proven that P = NP, and rumors are, the labs have cracked that one, too.

I told you things were going to keep getting wilder.

#### (1a) [ven chandrasekaran’s proposal for a quantum theory of vacuum gravity in 4d](https://x.com/tszzl/status/2125809711509336342?a=2Nt0lp_MiKNYiW3k2zs&s=20)

lol. It’s Thanksgiving week, so I wrote that line on probably getting even more important breakthroughs in the future on Wednesday. Then late that night, OpenAI employee and pseudoynmous man about the internet, roon, shared a link to his colleague’s proposal for a quantum theory of vacuum gravity in 4d .

And look, Ven Chandrasekaran got to use AI to help with the paper it, so for those interested in the details, I’m going to use AI to explain it:

> Chandrasekaran’s paper proposes a concrete, background-independent quantization of pure (vacuum) general relativity in four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime. It starts from first-order gravity, solves the constraints on intersecting null cones, defines a nonperturbative algebra of observables, builds a Hilbert space, and constructs a Lorentzian path integral. It then checks consistency by recovering known results: the Bekenstein-Hawking black-hole entropy (including the one-loop logarithmic correction that matches Sen’s earlier Euclidean calculation) and the semiclassical Einstein equations as the low-energy limit. In short, it is an attempt to turn general relativity itself into a quantum theory that passes the minimal tests any such theory must satisfy.

The paper still needs to be checked by other physicists (and their AIs, I’m sure), we’ll need to run experiments eventually to confirm, (per roon: “direct experiments for quantum gravity corrections that only show up at incredibly high energies are not possible with current technology), and it only covers vacuum for now, not matter. So we do not yet have an operational Theory of Everything, nor, based on early commentary I’ve seen from supergeniuses, is it likely that this will be the one when all is said and done.

But this is pretty exciting work for a Wednesday night.

#### (2) [U.S. Senate Passes Bipartisan Permitting Reform, BAAJA](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/19/climate/senate-passes-baaja-permitting-bill-energy.html)

[https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IIzu!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe3b9c10-2305-454f-890d-a321c41b9e8e_1400x787.jpeg](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!IIzu!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe3b9c10-2305-454f-890d-a321c41b9e8e_1400x787.jpeg)

Back to our regularly scheduled programming… We might need to grab WTFhappenedin2026 dot com. It could get a lot easier to build big things, from solar farms to subway lines, in the United States.

Last Friday, right after we hit send on last week’s Dose, the United States Senate passed big, bipartisan abundance legislation. By a 62–33 vote, the Senate approved the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA, H.R. 2347), sending the sweeping permitting reform package to the House this week with a bipartisan push to get it to the President’s desk before he heads off for Christmas at Mar-a-LaHoHoHo.

BAAJA would blast through the bottlenecks that make it so hard to build physical infrastructure in America by streamlining NEPA, Clean Water Act, and Endangered Species Act reviews. It includes dedicated provisions to accelerate electric transmission lines, nuclear energy licensing, energy permitting more broadly, and data center projects, all things we have cited as issues in the past in this very newsletter.

We can make all of the breakthroughs we want - new reactor designs, more generation, Riemann Hypotheses, etc… - but if it takes a decade to turn those things into physical infrastructure in the real world, we’re kneecapping ourselves from the benefits. BAAJA does a good job at making changes that will speed all of those things up without harming the environment. In many cases, it will help accelerate the transition to clean energy. The real losers on this one are those who like paperwork and dislike progress more than they actually like the environment.

We’re not quite there yet. The House needs to vote and the President needs to sign (hard to imagine him vetoing a bill for Builders, but who knows with this guy!). But getting 62 votes on something this big in the Senate is a great step and an important one in the march of progress.

#### (5) [Patient Marks One Year Living With Pig Kidney](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/well/pig-kidney-transplant-milestone.html)

Roni Caryn Rabin for The New York Times

[https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SUTZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c052f93-9d3a-425b-9d38-05cb0827e928_600x400.jpeg](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!SUTZ!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c052f93-9d3a-425b-9d38-05cb0827e928_600x400.jpeg)

One of the most fun parts of writing the Dose for as long as we have is that we’ve gotten to track stories as they’ve gone from sci-fi to early testing to early results to just a part of life. Like pig kidney xenotransplantation.

We've been tracking pig-to-human kidney transplants all the way from the earliest tests in brain-dead patients in [Dose #56](https://www.notboring.co/p/weekly-dose-of-optimism-56), through the first living human recipients and early milestones in [Doses #86](https://www.notboring.co/p/weekly-dose-of-optimism-86) and [#146](https://www.notboring.co/p/weekly-dose-of-optimism-146). On Sunday, November 22, the field crossed its biggest threshold yet: Molly Falco, a 58-year-old mother of two and former chef from New Hampshire, became the first person in history to live a full year off dialysis with a genetically engineered pig kidney, as [reported by](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/well/pig-kidney-transplant-milestone.html) [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/22/well/pig-kidney-transplant-milestone.html) and [announced by Mass General Brigham and eGenesis](https://news.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/first-patient-to-surpass-one-year-of-dialysis-independence-following-porcine-kidney-transplant).

Falco received her kidney (which she named **Lily**, after flowers her family in Colorado sent her) at Massachusetts General Hospital last November. Developed by George Church spinout eGenesis, the organ carries 69 (we made this joke in #146 already, but… nice!) genetic edits to make a Yucatan pig kidney play nice with the human body by inactivating pig retroviruses, knocking out three glycan antigens that trigger immediate rejection, and adding seven human genes to regulate immune response, inflammation, and clotting.

Before the surgery, Falco spent 11 grueling months on dialysis, too sick to even drive. Now, with her pig kidney, she hasn’t been hospitalized since February, is back to driving, and just got cleared to travel to Colorado this Christmas, where she’s planning on trying to ski the bunny slop with her kids!

“I’m amazed every day that it’s still working,” she told the NYT. “I’m going to try to keep it going as long as I can, and if that’s forever, that’s incredible.”

The pig kidney transplants are going to keep going, too. Of the six patients who’ve received them since 2024, three currently have them functioning (including Falco and two who received them this year), and eGenesis is launching formal trials in Q1 next year in 33 patients. We even have competition! Last Friday, United Therapeutics performed its [first pig thymo-kidney transplant](https://www.streetinsider.com/Corporate+News/United+Therapeutics+performs+first+pig+kidney-thymus+transplant+in+EXTEND+trial/27378743.html), and the company already has FDA clearance for a pig heart trial, too.

More than 500,000 Americans rely on dialysis (which has a five year mortality rate of 55%, worse than most common cancers), with more than 90,000 people on the kidney waitlist at any given time. Twelve Americans die waiting for a kidney every day. Thanks to our porcine friends, that may not be the case for much longer.

This is what it’s all about. We humans have a lot to be thankful for.
